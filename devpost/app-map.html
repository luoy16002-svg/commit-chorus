Commit Chorus — App Map

A snapshot guide to the finished proof of concept (source commit 5abc4c4). Paths and names can drift as the code changes.

What the app does

You pick a preset or type a public GitHub repository. The page reads its first year of commits, turns them into a score (every commit a note, every week a bar, regular contributors earning instruments), plays it with sampled instruments, and scrolls a music-box paper roll in step with the sound.

One action, end to end: pressing Play on the Deno preset

Reference route (three stops)

1

src/App.tsx → openPreset loads the preset file and calls show, which runs the arranger; toggle starts the sound engine on your click (browsers only allow sound after one).

2

src/music/arranger.ts → arrange is the heart of the app: it decides who plays which instrument (earnThreshold), which pitch each commit gets (step, a small melodic move chosen by the commit hash and kept inside the week's chord), and adds the bass and string chord.

3

src/audio/engine.ts → Engine schedules notes slightly ahead of time in pump; heardBeat tells src/roll/RollCanvas.tsx where the listener is, so holes light up when their notes are actually heard.

To change this, start here

  • The chord progression or the scale: CHORDS and PENTATONIC in src/music/arranger.ts.
  • How many commits earn an instrument: earnThreshold in the same file.
  • Instrument loudness and stereo position: MIX in src/audio/engine.ts.
  • Colours of each lane: VOICE_COLOR in src/roll/RollCanvas.tsx; page colours are CSS variables at the top of src/styles.css.
  • The presets: PRESETS in scripts/make-presets.ts, then npm run presets.

A practice to reuse

Look at real output before trusting a rule. The plan said a contributor earns an instrument on their third commit. The first screenshot of Deno's roll showed four of six instruments going to people who made three or four commits, while the main contributors played in "everyone else". The fix (a threshold that grows with the repository, earnThreshold) is recorded under Revisions in devpost/checklist.md and covered by tests/arranger.test.ts.

On another project: when a rule maps real data to something people will see, render it on a real dataset early and look, before building more on top of it.
